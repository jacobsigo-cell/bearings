# BEARINGS

**Know your ground.**

Bearings is a traveler-intelligence system built to identify, verify, and communicate destination conditions that can materially affect a trip.

Rather than relying on generic travel guidance, Bearings focuses on the rules, restrictions, policies, fees, access conditions, and other forms of visitor friction that travelers may not discover until arrival.

## Current Research Program

### Laguna Beach Research Set — LB-HOTEL-001

The initial Bearings research program examines hotel smoking policies and the practical effect of Laguna Beach, California's public-place smoking restrictions on visitors.

Research is conducted using a controlled evidence and verification methodology.

Findings are classified according to documented evidence rather than assumption, inference, or nonresponse.

## Research Principles

- Evidence before conclusion
- Source provenance
- Explicit verification status
- Reproducible findings
- Decision traceability
- Separation of observation from interpretation
- Correction when evidence changes
- Preservation of research history

## Evidence Lifecycle

Research generally follows this lifecycle:

**Discover → Capture → Verify → Classify → Review → Publish → Monitor → Correct → Archive**

Monitoring, correction, and archival occur when applicable.

A valid research package may contain zero qualifying EvidenceRecords. Research attempts, sources examined, and scope notes are retained even when qualifying evidence cannot be obtained.

## Governance

Bearings research is governed by the **Bearings Governance System** and **Bearings Verified Operations Manual**.

These establish requirements for:

- evidence capture;
- verification;
- source classification;
- finding status;
- research packages;
- decision traceability;
- review;
- publication;
- corrections; and
- audit history.

Changes to evidentiary status are recorded as auditable events.

## Repository Purpose

This repository contains the working technical and governance infrastructure for Bearings, including schemas, research records, documentation, and supporting assets.

The repository is intended to preserve a reproducible record of how published Bearings findings were reached.
